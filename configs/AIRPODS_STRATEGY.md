First establish which part is the body, which is the lid, and where the hinge
is, using overhead and wrist views. Keep a stable grasp on the body with one
arm. After lifting, inspect again: the case may pivot and the lid's position
relative to the fingers may change.

Use the other wrist view to locate the seam. Approach with open jaws oriented
across the lid's narrow depth. Keep the gripper housings separated and the
working fingers clear of the body-holding fingers. Adjust from measured poses
and visible alignment; each arm uses its own base frame.

Close on the lid only after the jaws visibly straddle it. Test with a small
opening motion. If only the fingers move, re-open, inspect the seam, and correct
the grasp rather than repeating the same pull. Follow the hinge arc upward and
backward as observed; pure vertical pulling may only crack the lid open.

Release the lid while maintaining body support, then inspect whether it stays
open. Call done only when the released lid remains visibly open. Describe the
evidence for progress or failure in each note, and reserve calls for this final
release-and-observe check.
